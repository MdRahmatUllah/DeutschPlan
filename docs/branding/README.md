# Branding

The name, the promise and the look for the app's next stage. **DeutschPlan**
names one course: German. The owner's direction (2026-09-26) is more
languages to learn and more meaning languages. So the app needs a name that
says "a clear path to a new language, in your own", whichever language that is.

This page covers the brand foundation, the naming criteria, the names checked
(with what the check found), the recommendation, and how one brand carries
many courses. The logo directions are in [`logo-concepts.md`](logo-concepts.md),
with sketches in [`concepts/`](concepts/).

These are proposals for the owner to decide. Nothing here changes the app yet.

## Why now

Renaming costs almost nothing today and a great deal later:

- The app is **not on Play yet** (the upload key is still to come), so there are no store reviews, rankings or users to carry over.
- The **application id can never change after the first upload** (`release.md`). Today it is `io.github.rahmatullah.deutschplan`, chosen on #170. It is rarely seen (the Play URL), but it is permanent. If the brand changes, decide the id in the same breath, before the upload.
- The launcher icon is still Flutter's default, so the icon has to be made anyway.

## The brand foundation

**Purpose.** Anyone can learn a language properly, from the first word to
fluency, in their own language, on the phone they already have, with or
without the internet.

**Promise.** A complete course with a plan for every day. You always know
what to do next, and you can see how far you've come.

**Who it is for.**
- First, Bangla speakers (Bangladesh, West Bengal, the diaspora) who need German for study, work, training or family reunion. Most need A1, B1 or B2 exam results (Goethe, telc), on mid-range Android phones, often with patchy or costly data.
- Then English speakers, and later Hindi, Urdu and Arabic speakers, learning German and other languages.

**What sets it apart** (from [`../handbook/01-business.md`](../handbook/01-business.md)):

| Pillar | What it means in the product |
|---|---|
| **A plan, not a game** | 12 steps from A1.1 to C2.2, a daily plan, FSRS revision, mock exams per step: structure over streak-chasing. |
| **In your language** | Every meaning in the learner's language, pronunciation in their script, the whole app in their language. |
| **Yours alone** | Offline, no account, no ads, no analytics. Progress stays on the phone. |
| **Exam-ready** | Mock exams with all five sections, including writing and speaking, and results by section. |
| **For everyone** | Text up to 200 %, screen readers, light, dark and glass themes, checked on every screen. |

**Personality.** A calm, patient tutor who takes you seriously: clear, warm,
encouraging, never childish, never nagging. Confident colours and a hand-made
"Paper & Ink" feel, not a cartoon mascot.

**Voice.** Plain words, short sentences, the learner as "you". Praise the work
("10 words reviewed"), not the person. Never guilt: a missed day becomes a
backlog, not a scolding. In Bangla, natural modern standard Bangla, as the
app's own copy already is.

## What a good name needs

1. **Says "language".** A stranger should guess it's about learning languages, or at least about words and speaking.
2. **Not tied to one language.** Nothing German (Deutsch, Sprach-) or Bangla-only.
3. **Easy to say for the audiences.**
   - Bangla speakers add a vowel before "st" ("istep") and merge z/j and v/bh.
   - Arabic has no /p/ ("path" drifts to "bath"), and "th" becomes s or t.
   - Hindi and Urdu are close to Bangla here.
4. **No bad meanings.** Checked in Bangla, Hindi, Urdu, Arabic, German, Spanish and French.
5. **Short.** At most 10 letters and 3 syllables, so it fits under a launcher icon (about 12 characters show) and in Play's 30-character title with a descriptor.
6. **Free to use.** No same-field app, company or trademark on it, and a usable domain (.com or .app).
7. **Fits the product.** "Step", "ladder", "plan", "path" or "way" match the 12-step course and the daily plan. **"Step" also matches the app's own word** for a level: *step*, ধাপ in Bangla.

## What the check found

On 2026-09-26, 48 names were checked in two rounds of web searches: the Play
Store, the App Store (through the web), companies and trademarks in the
field, the .com and .app domains, and how each sounds in Bangla, Hindi, Urdu,
Arabic and German. **This is not a trademark clearance** (see "Before choosing").

**The Lingo- family is out.** লিঙ্গ, pronounced "lingo" in Bangla, means
gender or sex and is also the word for the sex organ (Hindi and Urdu लिंग is
the same). For a Bangla-first brand that invites teasing. The root is also the
most crowded in the category (Duolingo, LingoDeer, Lingopie, Ling), and most
Lingo- and Lingua- names are taken.

**Bhasha- is weaker than it looks.** ভাষা/भाषा (language) is warm for
Bangla and Hindi speakers, but:
- "Bhashapath" (ভাষাপাঠ) is a standard West Bengal school grammar title, so the name is generic;
- Urdu says *zabān*;
- Arabic speakers can't say "bh" and hear "Basha" (the title "Pasha").

**How the Glot- names sound in German.** Beyond Glotstep, nothing is rude
or silly:
- **StepGlot** ends like *polyglott* (speaking many languages), which is positive;
- **Glotway** has no /ts/;
- **Glotplan** reads naturally, since *Plan* is German.

In Hindi and Urdu, "glot" means nothing. At most it is a distant pun on
*ghalat* (wrong), because the vowel differs.

**Fluent- is clear but descriptive.** People understand it at once. The same
fact makes it weaker as a trademark, and the space is crowded (FluentU,
Fluent Forever, Fluently). A compound like *Fluentladder* is suggestive
rather than descriptive, but counsel should confirm that it can be registered.

## The shortlist

Two routes: a name that **explains itself** (Fluent-, Talk-), or a name that
is **ours alone** ("Glot", from *polyglot*, someone who speaks many languages).
The Glot- names came back cleanest, but a store title and tagline have to
carry "Learn German" until people know the name.

| | Fluentladder | StepGlot | Glotway | Fluentmap | Glotplan | Talkladder |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Understood at a glance | **yes**: climb to fluency | "step" yes, "glot" via *polyglot* | via *polyglot* | yes | via *polyglot* | **yes** |
| Fits the product | **the ladder = the 12 steps** | **steps = the course (ধাপ)** | a way forward | the course map | **the daily plan** | speaking, levels |
| Ownable as a mark | weaker (descriptive root, crowded) | **strong** | **strong** | weaker | strong | fair |
| German ear | fine | **positive** (*polyglott*) | fine | fine | fine (*Plan*) | fine |
| Bangla, Hindi | fine; "fluent" is everyday English in South Asia | fine | fine | fine | fine | fine |
| Arabic | fine | "steb-glot", fine | **easiest** | fine | "blan", fine | fine |
| Letters | 12 (long for a launcher label) | 8 | 7 | 9 | 8 | 10 |
| Domains | .com and .app appear free | appear free | appear free | appear free | appear free | .app |

## The recommendation

**Fluentladder**, if people should understand the name without help, which is
the owner's brief. **StepGlot**, if owning the name matters more.

- **Fluentladder** says what the app does in two everyday English words, known well beyond English speakers: you climb to fluent.
  - The ladder is the course (12 steps, A1.1 → C2.2), and concept A's staircase draws it.
  - It holds for every future pair: Fluentladder German, Fluentladder French.
  - Its costs: 12 letters, which some launchers cut; a descriptive root, which makes the trademark weaker; and a crowded "Fluent" field.
  - Tagline: *Climb to fluent, one step a day.* In Bangla: *প্রতিদিন এক ধাপ, সাবলীলতার পথে* (one step a day, on the way to fluency).
- **StepGlot** is short, distinctive and clean everywhere.
  - The **step** is the app's own word for a level (ধাপ), and its logo.
  - In German it ends like *polyglott*.
  - Its cost: "glot" needs the tagline for a while.
  - Tagline: *Languages, step by step.* For the German course: *German, step by step*, and in Bangla *ধাপে ধাপে জার্মান*.

**Glotway** is the runner-up: the cleanest name found, and the easiest for
Arabic speakers. **Glotplan** keeps the "Plan" of DeutschPlan and the daily
plan.

## Before choosing

The check above is a web search. Before committing to a name:

1. **Trademarks:** search the finalists in the USPTO (tmsearch.uspto.gov), EUIPO (eSearch plus, TMview), WIPO's Global Brand Database, IP India, and Bangladesh's DPDT. Cover **class 9** (downloadable software) and **class 41** (education), and search the roots ("FLUENT", "GLOT") on their own as well.
2. **Stores:** search the exact name and near-spellings in the Play Console and in App Store Connect, which checks a name when you reserve it.
3. **Domains and handles:** check .com and .app at a registrar (WHOIS/RDAP), and the social handles.
4. **Legal:** a trademark attorney's clearance opinion before launch.
5. **People:** say the finalists to five Bangla speakers and five Arabic or Hindi speakers, and ask what they think the app does.

Then, in one issue before the first Play upload:
- the new name in both ARB files, the Android label and `store-listing.md`;
- the application id, if the owner changes it;
- the icon set (`logo-concepts.md`, "The icon specifications");
- the splash;
- a CHANGELOG line.
