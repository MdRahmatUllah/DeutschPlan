# German-language requirements by purpose of stay: official sources (for issue #1246)

Read on **2026-10-03**. Every page below was read on that date. Sources are official only:
make-it-in-germany.com (MIIG, the federal government's portal), auswaertiges-amt.de (AA),
the German Embassy Dhaka (dhaka.diplo.de), bamf.de, and goethe.de (Goethe-Institut Bangladesh).
None of the Dhaka embassy pages shows a "last updated" date. The two BAMF pages show their own dates (given below).

Quotes are verbatim, including the pages' own typos. "Not stated" means the page does not say it.

**At a glance.** MIIG's table, "Required German language skills depending on the type of visa"
(https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/living-in-germany/learn-german/knowledge):

| Type of visa | Required German (CEFR), quoted |
|---|---|
| Work visa for qualified professionals | "No legal requirement" |
| EU Blue Card | "No legal requirement" |
| Opportunity card (points system) | "German language skills at least at level A1 or English language skills at least at level B2" |
| Visa for the recognition of foreign qualifications | "German language skills at least at level A2" |
| Visa for employment within the framework of a recognition partnership | "German language skills at least at level A2" |
| Work visa for professionally experienced workers (incl. IT specialists) | "No legal requirement" |
| Visa for vocational training | "German language skills at least at level B1" |
| Visa for seeking a vocational training place | "German language skills at least at level B1" |
| Visa for studying | "As a general rule, German language skills at least at level B2 (depending on the study programme)" |

Under the table, the same page adds: "Please note: The above information on language requirements is based on the legal requirements in the Residence Act. Other language requirements may apply as part of any necessary recognition procedure or the examination procedure at the German missions abroad."

The Dhaka embassy does ask for more than the legal minimum in some categories (language course, Ausbildung). Those differences are flagged below.

## 1. Study visa

**Requirement:** It depends on the programme.
- **Taught in German:** you need proof of German. The level is set by the programme or university. MIIG says it is "usually" B2. The certificates the universities accept are DSH, TestDaF, telc Deutsch C1 Hochschule, DSD II and Goethe C2. For the visa, the Dhaka embassy *recommends* "Zertifikat B1" with a 'good' result or higher.
- **Taught in English:** "Generally" no German is needed (MIIG). Dhaka asks for proof of English (TOEFL, IELTS) instead.

## 2. Language course visa (Sprachkursvisum, § 16f AufenthG)

**Requirement:** The **law sets none** (MIIG: "No legal requirement"). The **Dhaka embassy**, however, asks for proof of German through a Goethe-Institut certificate, and it suggests "at least" B1 so that the application is plausible. The course must have at least 18 lessons (MIIG) or 18 hours (Dhaka) a week.

## 4. Job search: the Chancenkarte (Opportunity Card, § 20a)

**Requirement:**
- **Route 1, a qualification fully recognised in Germany:** no language proof is needed.
- **Route 2, the points system:** you need at least **German A1 or English B2**. German above A1 then earns points: **A2 = 1, B1 = 2, B2 or above = 3**. English C1 or above, or English as your native language, earns +1.

| same, option 2 | "Proof of language proficiency: You either have German language skills of at least level A1 as defined by the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR) or English language skills of at least level B2 (CEFR)." / "You must score a total of at least 6 points in the points system to receive an opportunity card." | same |

## 5. Skilled workers: the EU Blue Card (§ 18g) and the skilled-worker visa (§ 18a/18b)

**Requirement:** **No German is required by law** for either visa. Of the related visas, recognition-partnership and recognition visas need A2 (see the table at the top). A regulated profession can bring its own language rule through the recognition process. Healthcare, for example, needs B1 or B2 depending on the federal state (nurses); doctors need B2 plus a medical-language exam (Fachsprachprüfung) at about C1, set by the state chambers (Bundesärztekammer, https://www.bundesaerztekammer.de/en/work-and-training-in-germany/recognition; added from agent-0's review of #1397).

## 6. Family reunification (spouse)

**Requirement:** **A1, in general.** The Dhaka embassy requires it, including for spouses of German nationals. There are many **exemptions**: there is no A1 requirement if your spouse in Germany holds a Blue Card, an ICT Card, a skilled-worker permit (§18a/18b), a researcher permit, a self-employment permit, certain §19c permits, or certain settlement permits; for an illness or disability; and for some spouses of refugees. Children aged 16 to 18 who do not move with both parents need **C1**. Parents joining a child are exempt from the A1 rule.

## 7. The settlement permit (Niederlassungserlaubnis) and naturalisation (Einbürgerung)

### 7a. The settlement permit

**Requirement:** It depends on the route.
- **Skilled workers:** **B1**, after 3 years.
- **Blue Card holders:** **A1 after 27 months, or B1 after 21 months.**
- **Graduates of a German university or German vocational training:** **B1**, after 2 years.
- **Spouses of skilled workers:** **B1**, after 3 years.
- **The EU long-term residence permit:** "sufficient command of German", after 5 years. The CEFR level is not stated on this page.

Source: MIIG, Settlement permit, https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/living-permanently/settlement-permit

| Route (page heading) | Quote |
|---|---|
| Settlement permit for skilled workers | "You have held a residence title under Sections 18a, 18b, 18d or 18g of the Residence Act (AufenthG) for three years." / "You are able to demonstrate German language skills at level B1 of the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR)." |
| Settlement permit for EU Blue Card holders | "You have been in qualified employment for at least 27 months and have paid into the statutory pension insurance scheme." / "You are able to demonstrate German language skills at level A1 of the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR). If you can demonstrate knowledge of German at level B1 CEFR, the period is reduced from 27 to 21 months." (The Blue Card page agrees: https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/types/eu-blue-card) |

### 7b. Naturalisation

**Requirement:** **B1, after five years of residence.** The 2024 reform is stated on AA's page: since **27 June 2024**, naturalisation is possible after five years instead of eight, and you may keep your other citizenship.

**Caution: a three-year fast track.** The **English** BAMF page is older (dated "30/06/2025"). It still says: "If you have demonstrated a special aptitude towards integration – e.g. If you have displayed a particularly good academic or professional performance and possess very good German language skills – naturalisation is possible after just three years of residence in Germany." (https://www.bamf.de/EN/Themen/Integration/ZugewanderteTeilnehmende/Einbuergerung/einbuergerung-node.html). The **newer German** BAMF page (28.09.2026) and the MIIG page list five years and do not mention a three-year route. **It no longer exists:** the Bundestag voted on 8 October 2025 to delete § 10(3) sentence 2 StAG, and the change took effect on 30 October 2025; naturalisation is after five years only (agent-0's review of #1397, citing bundestag.de, *kw41 Staatsangehörigkeit*, and asyl.net; also https://hrrf.de/ende-der-turbo-einbuergerung-auch-bei-laufenden-verfahren/). The English BAMF page is outdated. Never mention a three-year route. The pages read do not give a CEFR level for "very good German".

## Goethe-Zertifikat exams: what each level tests (goethe.de)

| Level | Parts (verbatim) | Times | Notes | URL |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A1: Start Deutsch 1** | "The Goethe-Zertifikat A1: Start Deutsch 1 exam comprises reading, listening, writing and speaking sections. The speaking section of the exam is completed in a group." | Reading 25 min; Listening approx. 20 min; Writing 20 min; Speaking 15 min | "To reach this level, candidates need to have completed between 80 and 200 45-minute units of teaching". Recommended minimum age 16. | https://www.goethe.de/ins/bd/en/spr/prf/gzsd1/inf.html |
| **A2** | "The Goethe-Zertifikat A2 exam comprises reading, listening, writing and speaking sections." | Reading 30; Listening 30; Writing 30; Speaking 15 min | "between 200 and 350 45-minute classes". Recommended minimum age 16. | https://www.goethe.de/ins/bd/en/spr/prf/gzsd2/inf.html |
| **B1** | "The Goethe-Zertifikat B1 exam comprises reading, listening, writing and speaking modules. The speaking module of the exam is completed in pairs." / "The four modules can be taken individually or together, with four separate module certificates being the equivalent of the single all-round certificate." | Reading 65; Listening approx. 40; Writing 60; Speaking approx. 15 min | Also issued as the ÖSD-Zertifikat B1. "between 350 and 650 45-minute units of teaching". | https://www.goethe.de/ins/bd/en/spr/prf/gzb1/inf.html |

What each part asks you to do (verbatim):
- **A1.** Reading: "read texts such as brief notes, classified advertisements, signposts and posters and complete exercises on these texts." Writing: "fill in simple forms and write a short text about yourself on an everyday topic." Listening: "listen to short everyday conversations, telephone messages or public announcements over a loudspeaker and complete exercises on what you have heard." Speaking: "introduce yourself to the group before taking part in a question-and-answer session on everyday matters and asking someone in the group for something."
- **A2.** Reading: "read texts such as short newspaper articles, emails, advertisements and public announcement boards". Writing: "write messages related to your everyday environment." Listening: "everyday conversations, announcements and interviews on the radio, messages on the telephone and public announcements". Speaking: "pose and answer questions in conversations about yourself, converse about your own life and make agreements or negotiate with your conversation partner."
- **B1.** Reading: "read blog entries, emails, newspaper articles, advertisements and written instructions and pick out key points of information and important details as well as points of view and opinions." Writing: "write personal and formal emails and letters and write an online forum post in which you express your opinion." Listening: "announcements, short presentations, informal conversations and discussions on the radio". Speaking: "talk to your partner about an everyday topic, such as travelling (...) respond to questions, express your opinion and make suggestions. You will also be asked to give a presentation about an everyday topic and answer questions about it."

What the Goethe-Institut Bangladesh says each certificate is for (https://www.goethe.de/ins/bd/en/spr/prf/gzsd1.cfm and gzb1.cfm):
- **A1:** "obtain evidence of the language skills required to be granted a visa to join your husband or wife in Germany".
- **B1:** "attend a preparatory course (Studienkolleg) to qualify for admission to university in Germany", and "live and work in Germany".

telc exam pages were not read.

## The German Embassy Dhaka (and the Goethe-Institut Bangladesh): exams, certificates, waiting times

**Which certificates the embassy accepts.** The answer differs by visa type:
- **Family reunion:** "In general, we only accept certificates from Goethe Institute because there are no other language schools in Bangladesh that provide credible certificates. Provided that you passed the exam with another school not online but in person and the school has an ALTE certificate. We could also accept those certificates." (https://dhaka.diplo.de/bd-en/service/2689526-2689526) The proficiency page adds: "At present, the Goethe-Institut Bangladesh (German Cultural Centre) is the only institution in Bangladesh which can issue such certificates." (https://dhaka.diplo.de/bd-en/service/2685180-2685180)
- **Student visa:** "We accept TELC, TOEFL and IELTS certificates. Other certificates that are ALTE certified can also be provided. The certificates have to be obtained in person. Online certificates are not accepted in general." (https://dhaka.diplo.de/bd-en/service/2689586-2689586)
- **Opportunity Card:** German "at least A1! issued by Goethe Institute" (https://dhaka.diplo.de/bd-en/service/2685670-2685670).
- **Vocational training:** "Recognized proof of knowledge in German (Goethe Institute)" (https://dhaka.diplo.de/bd-en/service/2685226-2685226).
- **Language course:** "Proven through a Goethe Institute certificate." (https://dhaka.diplo.de/bd-en/service/2706884-2706884)
- **MIIG's federal list of providers accepted in the visa procedure:** "Goethe-Institut e.V.; telc GmbH; Austrian Language Diploma (ÖSD); TestDaF-Institut e.V. (...) level B2 and higher; ECL exam centres (...); German Language Diploma (...) (DSD); (...) DSH". The page adds: "Language certificates of other services are not recognised in the visa procedure." (https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/living-in-germany/learn-german/knowledge)

**Waiting times for visa appointments** (the embassy's own words):
- **Student visa:** "If you are a master or bachelor student without German or European scholarship, you should be aware of the constantly increasing waiting time that stands already now at more than 27 months." Also, on whether it will be reduced: "This is not to be expected." Students with a PhD or a German or EU scholarship ("qualified students") are processed first. (https://dhaka.diplo.de/bd-en/service/2685884-2685884)
- **Student visa FAQ:** "We are currently not able to give exact information about our waiting time." (https://dhaka.diplo.de/bd-en/service/2689586-2689586)
- **Language course visa:** "The waiting time is over one year. Exact estimates cannot be made." (https://dhaka.diplo.de/bd-en/service/2728870-2728870)
- **Family reunion:** "Expedited appointments cannot be granted." Processing takes "approx. 8 months" once the application is in. "A reduction in waiting time cannot be expected, unfortunately." (https://dhaka.diplo.de/bd-en/service/2684978-2684978, https://dhaka.diplo.de/bd-en/service/2754528-2754528) The FAQ says "the embassy cannot give you an exact information." (https://dhaka.diplo.de/bd-en/service/2689526-2689526)
- **Processing times after you apply.** The embassy gives two sets of figures that do not agree:
  - The National Visa page lists "Standard processing times": "Family reunion (including dependents and intended marriage): 12-16 months"; "Students (including preparatory (language) courses: 4 weeks"; "Hosting Agreement according to Section 18d German Residence Act and Blue Card: 3 weeks"; "Employment in general 4-6 weeks"; "Opportunity Card 8 weeks". ### Disagreements between sources (for the guide's wording)

1. **Ausbildung:** MIIG says B1 "normally"; Dhaka says A2 "usually" (B1 for medical professions). Suggested wording: "B1 is the usual rule; the embassy in Dhaka may accept A2 (but not for medical training)".
2. **Language course visa:** the law sets no minimum, but Dhaka suggests B1 with a Goethe certificate.
3. **Study, German-taught:** MIIG says "usually B2" (the university decides). Dhaka's visa checklist recommends at least "Zertifikat B1 'good'".
4. **Accepted certificates:** these vary by visa category at Dhaka (see above). The family reunion and Opportunity Card pages effectively mean Goethe.
5. **Naturalisation in three years:** abolished from 30 October 2025 (§ 7b). Only the outdated English BAMF page still mentions it.
